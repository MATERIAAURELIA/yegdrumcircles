Our approach

Why a facilitated drum circle?

A facilitated drum circle isn't a performance to watch or a tradition to learn. It's built around your group.

AspectTraditional drumming workshopFacilitated drum circle
FocusLearning set rhythms and their cultureYour group's connection and goals
The musicTaught by an instructorCreated together by the group, guided by a facilitator
Experience neededPractice helpsNone. Everyone succeeds in the first minutes
What people leave withAppreciation of a traditionListening, trust, leadership, belonging
FormatA set programCustom to your goals, with a debrief

How a team session runs

90 minutes

  1. Warm-up · arrive, rhythm games, everyone playing (10 min)
  2. Building the groove · one rhythm, created together (25 min)
  3. Leadership rotations · participants take the facilitator's spot (20 min)
  4. Creative challenge · call-and-response, small-group parts (15 min)
  5. Debrief · connecting the rhythm to how your team works (15 min)
  6. Closing rumble · one last big finish (5 min)

60 minutes

  1. Warm-up · rhythm games, everyone playing (10 min)
  2. Building the groove · created together (20 min)
  3. Leadership rotations · a few people take the lead (10 min)
  4. Debrief · what the circle shows about your team (15 min)
  5. Closing rumble (5 min)

What the research suggests

  • Burnout & mood. Research suggests a short group drumming program reduced burnout and improved mood in long-term care staff. Bittman et al., 2003
  • Rhythm builds teams. Studies have found that people who move in sync cooperate more afterwards and feel more on the same team. Wiltermuth & Heath, 2009
  • Older adults. Research suggests making music together, percussion included, supports mood, confidence and a sense of belonging in later life. Hallam & Creech, 2016

These are mostly small studies of group music-making, not studies of our sessions. Our circles are for wellness, connection and fun. They aren’t therapy or counselling, and they don’t replace professional mental health care.
